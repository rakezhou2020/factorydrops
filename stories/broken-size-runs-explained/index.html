CLEARANCE 101

Why Broken Size Runs Become Hard to Sell

A product can be perfectly usable and still become difficult inventory when the remaining sizes no longer form a complete retail assortment.

FACTORY DROPS EDITORIAL · AUGUST 2026

Retailers prefer complete assortments

A normal retail range works best when customers can choose among the expected sizes. Once popular sizes sell through, the remaining inventory may become much harder to merchandise.

This can happen even when every remaining item is new and fully usable.

A GOOD PRODUCT CAN BECOME BAD INVENTORY SIMPLY BECAUSE THE SIZE MIX IS WRONG.
Retail assortment depends on more than total unit count.

The remaining units may be scattered

Imagine a batch with plenty of XS and XXL but almost no M or L. The total unit count may still be significant, yet it is less attractive to a traditional retailer that wants a balanced range.

A broken size run may still contain perfectly useful goods.

Clearance channels solve a matching problem

Alternative channels can expose the remaining units to a wider audience rather than forcing one store to carry an awkward assortment.

For shoppers, the trade-off is simple: the deal may be attractive, but size and color availability can disappear quickly.

Alternative channels can help match scattered inventory with individual buyers.
